**Tube geometry notes.**

**Provenance for all four rows.** F-801 has not been pigged and no F-801 document states its geometry. Every row here is read from the **ExxonMobil F-802 inspection report** — its "Table 1: Exxon Baytown Tube Definition" for material, OD, wall and tube counts — and applied to F-801 on Jesse's statement of 2026-09-09: *"The inspection data I sent you was 802's inspection data just before they replaced the convection section. The 801 hasn't been pigged yet, but it should be identical to the 802."* F-802's convection was replaced April/May 2026, so that table records the **original** PS8 crude coil, which is what F-801 still has. **"Should be identical" is an expectation, not a measurement of F-801** — nothing on this card has been checked against F-801 itself, hence `verified: never`.

**All sections — `Sched` is `(not recorded)` deliberately.** ASTM A213 is a *tube* specification, sized by OD and wall thickness; a pipe schedule does not apply to it. The wall values above are stated on the source table, not derived. **Do not back-fill a schedule here** — F-802's card and SOP carry `6" Sch 80 / 5.761"`, and that Sch 80 assumption is the origin of every geometry error on that card.

**All sections — ID is computed, not stated.** ID = OD − 2 × wall, arithmetic on the table's stated values. 6.625 − 0.600 = 6.025; 6.625 − 0.750 = 5.875; 6.625 − 1.000 = 5.625. The report gives no ID column.

**Convection — segments 1-3, Avg Length is INFERRED.** Appendix A plots each convection tube against a 0–960 in axis (**80.0 ft**) with a mirrored scale (960→0 above, 0→960 below — the serpentine direction reversal), every tube spanning the full axis. Read as tube length that gives 6 × 80 = **~480 ft** of straight run per convection pass. The axis is not labelled as tube length, so this is an inference; the competing reading — 960 in as the whole unrolled path through the pass — would put tubes at ~13 ft and would not require a mirrored scale. Length/Circuit per segment is tubes × 80. **A stated convection tube length would retire this inference.**

**Convection — tube count is firm.** Appendix A labels tubes `1_1_C`–`1_6_C` and `2_1_C`–`2_6_C` with five return bends each (`1_1B_C`–`1_5B_C`), agreeing with Table 1's 3 + 1 + 2. **Six tubes per pass, not the twelve on F-802's card and in DSP26064.**

**Convection — segment 3 carries a different alloy.** Tubes 5-6 are A213 **T5** (5Cr-½Mo) where tubes 1-4 are T9 (9Cr). They are the outlet-end tubes (Appendix A marks INLET at tube 1, OUTLET at tube 6) and so the hottest, yet carry the lower-chrome alloy with the heavier wall. Recorded as the source states it. **Worth confirming against a coil drawing before it drives anything** — it could be as-built or a transcription slip in the report. Neither T9 nor T5 is stainless; both are ferritic chrome-moly, so no passivation follows from metallurgy.

**Radiant — Avg Length is the weakest figure on this card.** Table 1 gives 31 tubes but no length. The `~39 ft` is carried from F-802's `SOP-DCK-F802-001 REV 0`, whose convection figures are demonstrably wrong; its radiant length is not contradicted by the inspection data, but it is not independently sourced either. Length/Circuit is 31 × 39 and is therefore approximate twice over. **Do not treat ~1,209 ft as measured.** A coil drawing or a stated radiant footage retires this.

**Radiant — tube count is 31, not 30.** Table 1 states 31. F-802's card and SOP say 30.

**t_min.** The report gives a 0.250 in minimum wall on all four rows, stated as ExxonMobil-provided. Not a pig-sizing input — a thinned tube's ID grows, so the governing (smallest) ID stays 5.625 — recorded because it is the retirement threshold the customer inspects against.

**Max pig OD — the looped-circuit exception is NOT applied in the figure above.** 5.875" is the standard computed final. F-801 is looped at the radiant outlets, and `usadebusk-equipment` sanctions an oversized final above the standard figure for heavy fouling or looped circuits — its worked example runs 6.065" ID → 6.250" standard → **6.500" max**. F-802's `SOP-DCK-F802-001 REV 0` specifies **6.00"** and instructs upsizing in ⅛" increments until a 6.00" pig passes freely, and 6" pigs are on record for these heaters as far back as 2010. So 6.00" sits above the standard final but plausibly inside the exception. **The skill states the exception qualitatively with one worked example; extrapolating a ceiling for a 5.625" ID from it would be inference, so no larger figure is recorded here.** Jesse to rule before an F-801 SOP states a maximum.

## Notes

**Verification:** Nothing on this card has been checked against F-801 itself, which is why `verified: never`. What *was* checked: the tube counts, materials, ODs and wall thicknesses are read verbatim from ExxonMobil's F-802 inspection report, and Jesse confirmed on 2026-09-09 that the report predates F-802's convection replacement and that F-801 "should be identical." What pointedly was **not** checked: the report's own date and number (no copy on disk — Baytown inspection reports run `NN-NNNN-NNN`), any F-801 coil drawing, the radiant tube length, the convection tube length as a stated dimension, and the water supply arrangement.

**Related documents.** Quote [[DSP26064]] · facility [[02-facilities/ExxonMobil/Baytown-TX/_facility|ExxonMobil Baytown, TX]] · adjacent identical heater [[F-802]] (decoked as USA26022, 2026-04-28 to 05-07).

⚠ **[[F-802]]'s card disagrees with this one and F-802's card is the one that is wrong.** Both descend from `SOP-DCK-F802-001 REV 0`, which assumed 6" Sch 80 pipe and contradicts itself on footage (12 tubes × ~39 ft = 468 ft, printed above "228 ft per pass"). Against the inspection report it is wrong on convection tube count (12 vs 6), wall (one value vs three), metallurgy (A335 Gr P9 throughout vs A213 T9/T5), ID (5.761 vs 6.025/5.875/5.625), convection footage (228 vs ~480 ft/pass), radiant tube count (30 vs 31) and radiant wall (0.432 vs 0.500). **F-802's own correction is separate, Lane 4 work and has not been done** — and its two sections are different cases: its radiant is wrong outright (never replaced), while its convection is *unverified* rather than proven wrong, since that section was replaced in April/May 2026 and the replacement spec sheet has been outstanding since June.

⚠ **[[DSP26064]] reproduces F-802's incorrect geometry on its Technical Data page.** The card and the issued quote are expected to disagree: this card records the heater, the quote records what went to the customer. Per Jesse 2026-09-09 the quote stands as issued — **do not propose a revision.**

**Wall loss.** Jesse recalls wall loss in some of F-802's convection tubes, hedged, and expects F-801 may be similar — *"I think it will be. But I'm not sure."* Appendix A's C-scan shows the 0.300" nominal tubes 1-3 reading mottled against the 0.250" t_min while the 0.500" tubes read solid. Whether ExxonMobil expects comparable condition on F-801, and what inspection or retubing follows, is theirs to set. **Deposit composition, mechanism and metallurgical condition are never asserted from our work** — record what the customer states.

**Setup.** Identical to F-802 per Jesse 2026-09-09: ten 6" 300# RFWN bi-directional launchers at the control-valve station at grade, each its own launch and receive point with no separate receivers, and five facility-provided 6" 300# 180° loop spools at the radiant outlet flanges. F-802's circuit pairing was P2/P3, P1/P5, P9/P4, P10/P6, P8/P7. A 2010 F-801 temporary-facilities drawing retained in the USA26022 job folder shows an older north/south launcher-and-catcher arrangement and notes that jumpover spools "should be already fabricated and on-site from 2010" — the arrangement is superseded, but **the spools are worth confirming on site before rig-in.**

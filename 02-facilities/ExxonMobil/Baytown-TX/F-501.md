**Radiant.** 4 passes, 15 straight segments each. **Treat Gas.** Single continuous pass, independent of the process coils, and the only **carbon steel** section — the card previously recorded it as A335 Gr P9. ⚠ **Convection ID is LARGER than radiant ID here** (4.763" vs 4.635") — the condition
`usadebusk-core` flags as stop-and-confirm. It is genuine and confirmed by measurement, not a
data error. The mechanism is that all sections share one 5.563" OD and differ by **wall**, not by
pipe size: the radiant carries 0.464" wall against the convection's 0.400", so the heavier radiant
wall yields the smaller bore. The core heuristic is written around pipe *size* and does not hold
on a same-OD, mixed-schedule heater. Pig sizing is unaffected because it keys off the smallest ID
regardless of which section it sits in.

physical obstruction, not a tube-bore fact.

## Field Notes

### USA26041 — August 2026 HU5A TA

**Radiant bend replacement (ExxonMobil scope, permanent, completed before pigging).** ExxonMobil
removed the radiant plug headers (mule ears) and installed U-bends in their place, **and separately
cut off the bottom return bends and replaced those as well** (Jesse, 2026-08-15). Both were done
prior to pigging and both are **permanent**. The radiant serpentine carries 14 bends per pass —
7 top, 7 bottom — so **all 56 radiant return bends in the heater are steel installed during this
TA**, not original coil.

**Steady Flux and the ExxonMobil reps are both aware of the replacement** (Jesse, 2026-08-15).
Nothing below is an open action or a discrepancy to raise — it is recorded so that anyone reading
the report later reads the numbers correctly. The report computes every bend reading, and every
"short section of thin pipe," as loss from the *original* radiant nominal of 0.464":

- The report's headline **"Lowest Remaining Pipe Wall Thickness in Radiant Section = 0.252 in,
  45.7% loss" (D_4_1_R)** is one of seven short 6.4–7.4" segments described as having a larger
  internal radius, thinner wall, purposely welded in place, and located **near lower return
  bends** — which is exactly where the bottom bends were cut out and re-welded. These are almost
  certainly the new weld pups, i.e. new hardware measured against the wrong nominal, not coil
  degradation. Same applies to A_2_1_R (0.261"), B_4_2_R (0.357") and B_11_2_R (0.321").
- All 56 radiant bend rows in Table 8 describe the new bends, not the heater.

**The honest radiant condition number is A_15_R at 0.385" remaining, 17.1% loss** — the lowest
reading in normal, original radiant pipe. Do not let the 45.7% figure travel without this context;
read cold it says half the radiant wall is gone.

**Still valid and unaffected:** every straight-segment reading in the convection, radiant, cross-over
and Treat Gas sections, including B_8_C. Convection bends were not touched.

**Smart pig inspection — Steady Flux, 2026-08-13.** Tool: WiLBR Duroc, three units (SN002, SN009,
SN011), one per circuit, matching triple mode. Water temperature 107°F at inspection. Multiple runs
per section for full coverage. Lead technician Justin Ting; ExxonMobil contact Amanda Nelson.
Report `26-0663-002 Rev. A` issued 2026-08-15 — **~48 hrs after the run, against a spec sheet
promising a preliminary areas-of-concern report in 2 hrs and a final in 24**. Rig-out proceeded on
the preliminary; worth carrying as a scheduling actual, since `manual/14` gates rig-out on customer
acceptance of the inspection data.

**Inspection outcome.** Coil reported "generally clean with only minor fouling remaining" across all
three sections, no denting/swelling/bulging in any straight segment, and ovality never above 2.7%.
No areas of improperly-cleaned coke identified — a capability the WiLBR spec sheet lists explicitly.
That is third-party verification that the pigging met scope, and it is the most reusable artifact
this job produced.

**One segment warrants ExxonMobil's attention: B_8_C.** Convection Pass B, pipe 8 — **0.224"
remaining against 0.400" nominal, 43.9% loss, internal**, minimum at 195.7" from the upstream weld
at 210° from TDC (7:00 clock position), with loss extending nearly the full 226" segment. Ovality a
normal 2.2%, so not mechanical. Internal, bottom-of-pipe, full-length in a horizontal tube. It is
the only such segment in the heater — the next-lowest normal convection reading is A_3_C at 0.376"
(6.1%). ExxonMobil supplied **no minimum allowable wall threshold**, so the report makes no
fitness-for-service call; that assessment sits with ExxonMobil.

**Section condition summary (normal pipe only).** Convection 0–6.1% loss · Radiant 0.3–17.1% ·
Treat Gas a uniform 9–14.5%, min 0.321" at A_7_TG. The Treat Gas pattern is general thinning with
no localised anomaly, consistent with it being the one **carbon steel** section against 9Cr
elsewhere.

**requesting the bore profile for A_1_X and B_1_X would characterise the restrictions precisely**
and is the cheapest way to size Passes A and B correctly next time.

convection (4.763"), and their appendages were worn down by the time they arrived — which made the
convection tubes slightly harder to clean than their bore alone suggests (Jesse, 2026-08-16). A
launch-direction consequence on this rig configuration, not a heater defect.

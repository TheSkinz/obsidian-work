## Notes

**Verification.** `verified: never` is correct and deliberate. **Stated on a 100-H-2 drawing:** every figure in the Shell and Elevations block, the 40'-0¼" radiant tube length, the 6" Sch 40 crossover, the presence of 8" radiant tubes and returns at four places, and the four-pass count (Section B is labelled "four places"). **Everything in Tube Geometry and Config Rollup is either carried from SteadyFlux or absent.** No convection view, no tube schedule and no tube count has been read for this heater.

**Why the sister-heater assumption is not written into the tables.** SteadyFlux's table gives 100-H-1 and 100-H-2 as identical in every cell, which is a copy signature rather than two independent derivations — and their 100-H-1 row proved 21% high on footage once the Quest coil map was read. Inheriting a figure from a row that is known wrong, via a table that copied it, is two unsupported steps. The 2026-08 estimate used 5,122 ft for planning and said so; the card does not adopt it.

**What would close this.** A convection elevation or tube schedule for 100-H-2, or the equivalent of 100-H-1's Quest FTIS coil map. Failing a drawing, a field count of convection tubes per pass and radiant tubes per pass — counting U-bends and adding one is the reliable method on a stacked serpentine.

**2019 retube.** `CN-19-708-10-03` is a demo sheet from the same GST job (CN-18-708) that retubed 100-H-1, and it shows the same work: demolish the existing 8" tubes, 8" return and 8×6 reducer at four terminal locations. Section B also notes the existing 6" Sch 40 return may need removing and reinstalling. Companion sheet `10-04` is referenced and was not supplied.

**2026-08 estimate basis** (not an actual). Carried as identical to 100-H-1: two circuits at 2,561 ft each, double mode, 80 ft/hr, 30% parallel allowance, rig-in 4 / smart pig 4 / rig-out 4, landing at 60 hrs / 5 shifts. That rests on the sister-heater assumption above and moves if the real geometry differs.

## Pigging Scope and Method — RESOLVED

Jesse, 2026-07-27: **"the two process coils"** = the two passes **in full**. Both sections are
in the pig path, in flow order — Convection → crossover (concentric reducer) → Radiant.

**Method for the two-bore coil** — settled practice, not a per-job decision. The smaller
convection bore is pigged through from its own connection. The larger radiant section is then
pigged from the launcher/receiver installed on **the larger-size flange**: pigs are launched
into the radiant and run until they reach the reducer, at which point flow is lost; direction is
then reversed and the pig is pushed back out to the same launcher/receiver it came from.

Consequences worth stating, because they were open questions earlier and are now closed:

- **No crossover break is required.** The welded crossover stays welded.
- **4 spools is the correct count** — one launch/receive point per end of each circuit, two per
  pass. A crossover break would have wanted 8.
- **The reducer is a backstop, not an obstruction.** It is what tells the crew the pig has
  reached the end of the radiant run.

The bore step is substantial: 4.625" convection to 5.875" radiant, **1.250" on diameter**. A
convection-legal 4.875" pig never touches the wall in a 5.875" bore, so the radiant runs are not
optional refinement — they are the only thing that cleans the radiant. Radiant pigs go up to
**6.125"**, which is why they must be launched from the radiant-side connection.

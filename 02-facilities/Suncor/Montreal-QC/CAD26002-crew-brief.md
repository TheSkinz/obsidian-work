The job, as they define it (§.1): *"To decoke furnace tubes of B101, B102, B103, and B1001 and B151 preheater as per guide GM-07-000-00-001 and per the Montreal Quality Control Manual requirement."* Note they count it as **four furnaces and one preheater**, and they call B-151 the preheater throughout.

## 1. Pigs — the prohibition

GM-07, Pigging Step, step 2 (repeated verbatim as step 7 in the soda-ash annex, so it applies whether or not soda ash is in use):

> *"Be sure to use only urethane coated foam pigs. The use of pigs with tungsten carbide tips is prohibited. Use pig with square head 600 BHN hardened steel tips or stainless steel hex head bolts. For 600 BHN pig, use only 3/16" diameter square heads. 1/16" Heads are prohibited (see photo). More abrasive pig like those with tungsten carbide tipped studs have been shown to severely damage 180 degree elbows."*

DI-GE-5-007 §.4 repeats it in the contractor equipment list, in caps:

> *"Cleaning pigs of different diameters suited for furnace tubes. The use of pigs with tungsten carbide tips is PROHIBITED"*

Check the load before we run anything. The same list also requires:

> *"Foam witness pigs to determine the diameter of the first cleaning pig"*
> *"The final pigs of adequate size to confirm the cleanliness of the tubes"*
> *"Swab pigs to adequately dry furnace tubes"*

## 2. When the tube is clean — and the instruction to stop

GM-07, step 3:

> *"When using pigs of equal diameter to the pipe to remove deposits, record the duration (in seconds) of black effluent water circulation after the pig has reached reception point. The data should be entered in the pigging log. This parameter has been shown to be the best indicator of tube cleanliness."*
>
> *"A return of black effluent lasting 6 to 8 seconds followed by a jet of gray or rust-colored water indicates a clean tube. Pigging must be stopped when this criterion is met to avoid creating furrows in the tubes."*

That is a stop instruction, not just a record. The seconds go in the pigging log for every run.

## 3. What they require us to document and have signed

GM-07, steps 4 through 8:

> *"If a filtration system is used, a new bag or filter element should be installed at the start of each pigging. Before using the bag or filter element, the decoking team should record the dry weight."*
>
> *"Estimate and record the amount of deposits removed. Due to hardware limitations, this estimate is usually obtained by measuring the volume."*
>
> *"Before running the last pig for each bundle, visually inspect it by the Suncor execution group. Note that, in the case of carbon steel tubes, the white foam pig are often gray in color on the return."*
>
> *"After pigging, have the cleanliness checklist and pigging log signed by the Suncor operating group. The initial and final hydraulic tests must be carried out at 3 different flow rates."*
>
> *"Perform a visual inspection of the visible part of the tubes when removing the launchers. Report any damage to the tubes."*

Samples, if their process engineering department asks for them: *"Take representative samples of the deposits removed… Indicate on the label of each sample the type, date and time of collection, the number of the device and the number of the tube bundle."*

The hydraulic tests are also in the directive, DI-GE-5-007 §.4:

> *"Make an initial hydraulic test to assess the condition of the tubes and final after the end of the decoking work"*

## 4. Soda ash — B-102 radiant

DI-GE-5-007, particular conditions on furnace B-102:

> *"DECOKING WITH SODA ASH AND TUBE DRYING WITH NITROGEN for radiant only"*

Their technical data table (§.6) prints the soda ash volume as **21000 GAL** for B-102 and *"N/A — NO SS IN TUBES"* for B-101, B-1001, B-103 and B-151. **B-102 radiant is the only place soda ash is used on this job.** Still worth a sentence with the rep at kickoff so they say it out loud, but this is our position, not an open question.

Where it is used, GM-07 Annexe F sets the chemistry, and it is monitored:

> *"Prepare a 2wt% solution of soda ash (Na2CO3)… The water used must have a Cl concentration <50 ppm or less… In the freshly produced soda ash solution, the Cl concentration should be <65 ppm and should have a pH> 9."*
>
> *"Circulate the solution for 1 hour in each direction after connecting the process piping."* — their note: *"Total of 2 hours minimum"*
>
> *"Inject the soda ash solution with a temperature <130 ° F."* — their note: *"Above 130 ° F the risk of chloride cracking increases significantly."*
>
> *"30 minutes after starting the treatment, the soda ash solution must be analyzed and thereafter every 2 hours (MAX) for the total duration of the decoking."* — limits: *"pH >9. Cl < 250 ppm. Kitagawa tubes can be used to test for Cl."*

Out of spec: *"If the Cl concentration is too high, correct the parameter within the hour… If the pH limit is not respected, add soda ash to restore the pH. Correct the parameter within the hour."*

On foaming, under their own WARNING heading:

> *"During the circulation operation, it may form foam. Add 'raw' diesel as an anti-foaming agent until the situation recovers. Use approximately 1 gallon at a time. If there is a risk of overflow, lower the level using a vacuum truck."*

Suncor supplies the diesel (§.7).

## 5. Nitrogen drying — their safety protocol, verbatim

GM-07, Drying Step, *"Prior to the drying step"*:

> *"Provide two single frequency radios. One will be assigned to the operator positioned at the control valve on the nitrogen network and the other to the technician in the cab of the pigging truck;"*
>
> *"Close the windows of the pig truck cab, prevent personnel from accessing the recovery tank and turn off any systems that could allow nitrogen to enter the interior of the pig truck cabin (heating, air conditioning);"*
>
> *"Establish a safety perimeter around the pigging truck to limit the presence of personnel not essential for this task;"*
>
> *"Each worker entering inside the perimeter must be equipped with a gas detector. This minimizes personnel in the area. If a gas detector rings, personnel in the cabin and in the area must be evacuated and the nitrogen valve must be closed immediately. The presence of an operator at the nitrogen valve is therefore mandatory at all times."*

On the purge itself, step 15:

> *"Completely purge the soda ash solution with factory nitrogen to the pigging truck with the swap. It is mandatory to stop the purge as soon as the draining of the liquid (soda ash) is completed in the truck, a loud gas whistling will be heard during the transition between liquid and gas. Communication must take place by single frequency radio. If the piggin is done for a vertical furnace, run the sponge pig 3 times with nitrogen."*

Their §.6 table lists **B-103 tube position as VERTICAL** (with *"HORIZONTAL ON 24 FT FOR 4""*), and B-1001 with an exception position of *"VERTICAL ON 12 FT FOR 4""*. So the sponge-pig-three-times rule is live on this job.

Drying, steps 21 through 29:

> *"If the furnace tubes are vertical, complete the drying of the tubes with hot nitrogen at ≈130 ° F"* — their note: *"It is very important not to exceed 140 ° F in this situation"*
>
> *"Dry for at least 30 minutes."*
>
> *"Target for a dew point of 40 ° F below the wall temperature of the tubes"*
>
> *"Validate the dew point with Draeger type company water vapor concentration analysis tubes"* — their note: *"This test a GO / NO-GO."*
>
> *"Complete the test at the nitrogen outlet located at the upper level of the furnace."*
>
> If not reached: *"Reverse the nitrogen flow and test after 1 hour."*
>
> *"Drain all possible low points in order to eliminate the last traces of alkaline solution."* — their note: *"The goal is to avoid cracking by Cl or by alkaline solution."*
>
> *"Keep the furnace tubes at low nitrogen pressure following drainage."*

DI-GE-5-007 adds, for B-102: *"With the drying pig (SWAB), flush with nitrogen at the end to get the water out, then disconnect the hose from the truck and vent it in a safe place… (to be coordinated with Suncor operation / execution)"*

**Ours** (§.4, *"Scope of work – supplied by contractor"*), beyond the pigs listed above:

> *"Pumper truck(s), filter press and hoses required for decoking"*
> *"Launchers / receivers for the 4 furnaces and 1 preheater with in particular having for the B-102 launchers and receivers with vents or drains of 2 inch for decoking the radiant section so that we can connect a nitrogen line to dry the tubes."*

Note that last parenthesis — they have decoked these units in 2018 and 2021, so the crews on their side have done this before.

Two things to check at the flange before building anything: **B-101 radiant is called 150#**, stated twice in their documents, and **B-102 circuit #2 carries 4" convection tube behind an 8" launcher** — so the 4" governs the pig on that circuit, not the radiant bore.

Common to all five, from their technical data table: **maximum decoking pressure 740 PSIG**, ambient temperature during decoking 100 °F.

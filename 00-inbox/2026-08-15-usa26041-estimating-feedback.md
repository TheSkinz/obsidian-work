# USA26041 estimating feedback — three items from the first F-501 job

Captured at close-out 2026-08-15 from the ExxonMobil Baytown F-501 decoke (USA26041). All three are
model-level, not job-level, so none of them belong on the heater card. Detail and arithmetic live on
[[USA26041-job-sheet]] and [[F-501]].

**The $103.50 blended pig unit is high.** DSP26071.2 quoted 25 pigs at $103.50 each ($2,587.50 cost,
$2,975.63 with the 15% mark-up). The job actually ran **43 pigs for $2,742.20** ($3,153.53 marked
up) — eighteen more pigs for about $178 more. Real unit costs from the receipts and the ticket
breakdown: 6" swab $8.60 · 4.75" gauge foam $31.90 · 5" gauge foam $35.40 · 4.25" pin $64.90 ·
4.5" pin and HR $73.20 · 4.75" pin and HR $81.40. The line over-recovers on count and the count is
the thing that varies. Worth rebuilding the pig unit off a real size mix rather than a single
blended figure.

**Rig-out was under-estimated by 75% and needed a second shift.** Quoted 8 hrs, actual 14 — split
8 hrs on receipt 10784 and 6 hrs on 10785, so it did not fit the single block it was priced in.
Pigging went the other way: 16 actual against 24 quoted, a third under, on a coil the inspection then
confirmed clean. Net 43 productive hrs against 48 quoted, which hides the shape entirely. The scope
was six launchers, three circuits in triple mode, filtration and a smart pig — that is the profile
to carry the rig-out correction into. n=1 and Condition is `first`, so it does not move the routine
baseline.

**Per diem should be priced off calendar days on site, not shift count.** The quote assumed 6 people
× 2 days = 12 person-days. The job ran 4 calendar days, so 15 T&M person-days billed plus the mob
and demob days inside their lump sums. Per diem was always going to overrun a 2-day assumption on a
job whose own execution plan spanned more than that.

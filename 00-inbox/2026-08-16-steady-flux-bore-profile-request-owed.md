Open action carried out of the USA26041 close-out, 2026-08-16. **Superseded by the
retirement block above** — the record below is kept as written.

**What.** Request the internal-radius / bore-profile data for cross-over segments **A_1_X and
B_1_X** from Steady Flux Technologies, against inspection `26-0663-002 Rev. A` (run 2026-08-13,
lead technician Justin Ting).

**Why it is worth a request rather than a shrug.** Passes A and B topped out at a **4.75" final
pig** against the **4.875"** computed from the 4.635" radiant ID, because of metal restrictions
protruding into the tubes. Those restrictions were located and mapped **from the Trimax digital
chart recorders** — a pressure/position signature — and were never visually identified, because
the cross-overs were not opened. Steady Flux's UT readings show nothing anomalous there
(A_1_X 0.458", 1.3% loss, 1.2% ovality) and that is expected: UT measures wall, not bore
obstruction. But the report states on p. 4 that they *do* gather internal-radius dimensional data
in exactly these areas — so the measurement that would characterise this may already exist in
their raw data.

**What it buys.** It converts "plan Passes A and B against 4.75"" from a defensive assumption into
a measured number, and it tells ExxonMobil whether there is something in that piping worth
addressing. Cheapest possible answer — a data request against an inspection already paid for, not
a new mobilization.

**Decays.** Vendor raw data and technician recall are freshest now. The value drops the longer it
sits, and the next F-501 scope is years out.

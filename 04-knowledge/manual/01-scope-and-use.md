## 1.1 Scope

This manual covers mechanical decoking of fired heater coils performed by propelling cleaning pigs through the coil with water. It applies to convection sections, radiant sections, and the cross-over piping between them, on heaters in refinery and chemical plant service.

The following are within scope and covered in Part 4: filtration and effluent handling, initial flush and pitch or oily water removal ahead of decoking, water propulsion support for smart pig and in-line inspection tool runs, and the mechanical portion of stainless steel work where customer-performed passivation follows cleaning.

The following are outside the scope of this manual. Chemical cleaning, steam-air decoking, and hydroblasting are separate methods and separate scopes. Lifting and rigging is performed by the customer's lifting contractor. Inspection data acquisition and interpretation is performed by the inspection vendor.

## 1.2 How this manual relates to the job SOP

Every USADebusk decoking job is executed against a heater-specific execution SOP, numbered `SOP-DCK-[HeaterTag]-[NNN]`, prepared before mobilization and issued to the customer. That document states the actual circuit configuration, tube dimensions, flange connections, pig progression sequence, operating parameters, and any facility-specific requirements for that heater.

This manual states the method those SOPs apply. Where the two describe the same activity, the job SOP governs, because it carries the confirmed heater data. Nothing in this manual overrides a job SOP, a customer procedure, or a site permit condition.

## 1.3 How to read it

Parts 1 and 2 establish shared vocabulary and set out what USADebusk needs from the customer to engineer the job. A client engineer preparing a scope of work will find Section 4 the most directly useful chapter in the manual.

Part 3 follows the job in execution order. Part 4 covers services that attach to a decoking scope but are not part of every job; each is identified as elected or conditional, and none is assumed.

Part 5 carries the deliverables list, glossary, and reference tables.

## 1.4 Units and conventions

Dimensions are in inches, flow in US gallons per minute, pressure in PSI, and velocity in feet per second unless stated otherwise. Tube sizes are given as inner diameter where the figure governs pig sizing, and as outside diameter with schedule where the figure is used to verify inner diameter against a drawing.

Coil entry and exit points are referred to as inlets and outlets throughout.

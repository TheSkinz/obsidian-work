# 6. Safety and Permit Interface

**Layer:** 04-knowledge/manual
**Source:** `04-knowledge/sops/sop-formatting-standard.md`, `~/.claude/skills/usadebusk-sop/SKILL.md`
**Manual:** [[00-manual-index]]

USADebusk works to the host facility's safety management system. Where anything in this manual conflicts with a site permit condition, a facility procedure, or a direct instruction from the site authority, the site governs.

## 6.1 Personal protective equipment

Minimum PPE for all personnel in the work area is hard hat, ANSI-rated safety glasses with side shields, flame-resistant clothing, steel-toe boots, and gloves appropriate to the task. Face shields are worn for connection breaking and any task with splash exposure. Hearing protection is worn in the pumper working area and wherever the site requires it.

Site-specific requirements above this minimum, including respiratory protection, gas monitoring, and any facility-mandated equipment, are identified in planning and stated in the job SOP.

## 6.2 Permit and authorisation interface

Work does not begin until the permit to work is issued by the facility and a job safety analysis has been completed and reviewed with the crew performing the work. The JSA is reviewed again at each shift change and whenever the scope, conditions, or crew composition change.

Simultaneous operations are identified before mobilization. Where other work is proceeding in or around the heater at the same time, the interaction is addressed in the permit and in the JSA rather than managed informally in the field.

## 6.3 Isolation and mechanical readiness

The coil must be isolated, drained, and made safe by the facility before USADebusk connects to it. Positive isolation and blinding, protection or isolation of relief devices, and confirmation that the coil is free of process fluid and at safe temperature are customer scope, covered in Section 7.

> **WARNING.** No connection is made to a coil until the facility has confirmed isolation is in place and the coil has been released to USADebusk under permit.

## 6.4 Pressure control

The system operates at 150 to 300 PSI under normal conditions, against a maximum of 600 PSI. Pressure is monitored continuously at the operator station for each circuit.

The system is filled and pressure tested at rig-in before any pig is launched. That test verifies the integrity of every connection made, and no launch occurs until it is satisfactory.

Rising pressure during a pass is expected behavior, not automatically a fault: a pig meeting resistance in a fouled section will show a pressure rise, and pressure relaxing as the pig clears is the normal signature. What is controlled is the ceiling. Above 500 PSI the operator works to a defined over-pressure checklist rather than continuing to raise output, and 600 PSI is an absolute limit.

> **CAUTION.** Pump output is never raised beyond the defined limits to force a pig through an obstruction. Where a pig will not advance under normal control, the response is the procedure written into the job SOP, not additional pressure.

## 6.5 Connection integrity and depressurization

Every connection is a pressure boundary. Connections are made up and checked before the system is filled, and personnel do not stand in line with a launcher lid, receiver lid, or hose end while the system is under pressure.

> **WARNING.** The system is depressurized through the bleeder valves and confirmed at zero pressure before any connection is broken, any launcher or receiver lid is opened, or any hose is disconnected. This applies at every pig change, not only at rig-out.

## 6.6 Effluent and product handling

Returning effluent may carry hydrocarbon, coke fines, and residual process material. It is handled as a potentially contaminated stream from the moment it leaves the receiver, contained within the system, and routed only to the disposal point the facility has designated. Section 12 covers waste handling.

## 6.7 Access and work at height

Where launcher or receiver locations are elevated, a sound working deck with room to change pigs is required at the working position. Where that does not exist, scaffolding is required and is provided by the customer. Fall protection requirements are set by the facility.

USADebusk performs no lifting or rigging. All lifts are performed by the customer's lifting contractor.

## 6.8 Continuous operation

Decoking runs continuously with shift handovers. Handover includes circuit status, pig progression to date, current operating parameters, effluent condition, and any anomaly observed on the preceding shift. Continuity of the record across handovers is part of the procedure, not an administrative extra, because pig progression decisions depend on what the previous passes did.

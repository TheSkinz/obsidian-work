# 13. Initial Flush and Pitch / Oily Water Removal

## 13.1 When this phase applies

Some coils are released to USADebusk still holding residual hydrocarbon. A coil that carried heavy or viscous product, or one whose deinventory was incomplete, contains material that will mobilize as soon as water is introduced.

Where that is the case, an initial flush phase precedes mechanical decoking. It is a distinct phase with its own effluent handling, and it is identified in planning rather than discovered when the first pig comes back.

The condition is common on crude and coker service, and on units where pitch is the expected fouling variant. It is agreed with the facility as part of the scope, because the material being removed is process material and its disposal is a facility decision.

## 13.2 Why it comes first

Two reasons.

The first is effluent segregation. Hydrocarbon-bearing water is a different waste stream from coke-laden decoking effluent, and the two are handled differently. Removing the hydrocarbon first keeps the streams separate and keeps the decoking effluent within what the designated route can accept.

The second is equipment protection.

> **CRITICAL.** Oily water is prohibited from entering the filter press. The press separates solids from water; hydrocarbon blinds the filter cloths and fouls the plates. Where an initial flush is performed on a job with filtration elected, the flush effluent is routed to the designated disposal point and the filtration loop is placed in service only once the stream has been confirmed free of hydrocarbon.

## 13.3 Method

The coil is flushed with water through the same circuit configuration used for decoking. Effluent is routed to the designated disposal point, generally the facility's oily water system or a vacuum truck taking suction on the dirty tank, and is not recirculated.

Flushing continues until the effluent runs free of hydrocarbon. That determination is made by observation and, where the facility requires it, by the facility's own sampling.

Where the residual is heavy or has hardened, a chemical additive is used to mobilize it. Kicksolve is the additive used for hardened residual product and pitch. Its use is agreed with the facility in advance, since it introduces a chemical to the coil and to the effluent stream, and the product data is included in the pre-execution package where the facility's review requires it.

Foam pigs may be run during the flush to assist displacement. These are displacement passes, not cleaning passes, and they are recorded as such so the pig progression record for the decoking phase starts from a clean baseline.

## 13.4 Transition to decoking

Three things are confirmed before mechanical decoking begins.

Effluent runs free of hydrocarbon. The disposal of the flush stream is complete and, where filtration is elected, the loop is clear to be placed in service. And the baseline flow test is run, or re-run, on the flushed circuit, so that the decoking baseline reflects the coil as it stands at the start of cleaning rather than as it stood full of product.

## 13.5 Effect on the decoking scope

A coil that required an initial flush is generally one carrying heavier fouling, which bears on pig selection. Harder-durometer pigs are more likely to be used, and the progression may need more passes at each increment. Both are reflected in the job SOP once the flush has shown what the coil is actually holding.
